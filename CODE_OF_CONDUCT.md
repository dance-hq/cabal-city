# Code of conduct

Cabal is built for people who meet each other in real life: at the next meetup, the next demo night, the next coffee. Treat everyone here the way you'd want to be treated by someone you're about to run into.

This code covers this repository and everything around it: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and commits. It also covers anyone speaking for the project somewhere else. Inside a Cabal community, its own [community guidelines](https://www.cabal.city/guidelines) apply, and they say the same things.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good intent. Disagree with ideas, not people.
- Give feedback on the work, specifically and usefully. Take feedback the same way.
- Help newcomers. Everyone here started by not knowing how something worked.
- Credit other people's work and ideas.
- Own your mistakes, apologize to the people they affected, and learn from them.

## What isn't okay

- Harassment, threats, or insults, in public or private.
- Discrimination or hate based on who someone is: their race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, age, body, appearance, experience level or background.
- Sexual language, images or attention, anywhere, and anything involving minors.
- Sharing someone's private information, messages or contact details without their permission.
- Trolling, deliberately derailing a conversation, or repeatedly pushing an issue after maintainers have made a decision.
- Spam, self-promotion with no part in the conversation, or recruiting for anything illegal.
- Pretending to be someone else.
- Anything else a reasonable person would see as out of line in a professional setting.

## Reporting

If someone makes you uncomfortable or breaks this code, email **[matt@cabal.city](mailto:matt@cabal.city)**. Say what happened, where, and include links or screenshots if you have them. You don't need to be sure it counts before writing.

Reports are private. Only the maintainers who handle them see them, and we won't share who reported something without permission. If the report is about a maintainer, that person won't take part in handling it.

Security vulnerabilities go through [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md) instead.

## What maintainers may do

Maintainers decide what's acceptable and act fairly and in proportion. Depending on what happened, that can be:

1. **A private word.** An explanation of what was wrong and what to do differently.
2. **A warning.** A clear note, with consequences if it happens again.
3. **A temporary ban.** No interacting with the project for a set time.
4. **A permanent ban.** For repeated problems or a single serious one, such as harassment or threats.

Maintainers may also edit or remove comments, commits, code and issues that break this code, and they'll say why when it's safe to.
